ANSWER:
Chunking is a critical part of the ingestion stage in a RAG pipeline, where documents are split into smaller pieces (chunks). These chunks are then embedded into vectors and stored in a vector database. The size of the chunks is important; if they are too large, they can contain multiple topics, blurring the embedding, and if too small, they can lose context. A common approach is to use chunk sizes of 256 to 512 tokens with 10 to 20 percent overlap between them.

ANSWER:
Chunk overlap is a common approach in the RAG ingestion stage, typically 10 to 20 percent of the chunk size. It is important because it prevents information from being cut off at chunk boundaries. If a sentence spans two chunks, overlap ensures the full meaning appears in at least one chunk. Without overlap, retrieval quality drops on queries that depend on cross-boundary context.
